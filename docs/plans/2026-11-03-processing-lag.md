# Processing lag: how long a drive takes to process, and how far behind real time we run

Status: step 1 **deployed** (2026-11-03; metrics reach Prometheus, lag figures wait for a drive),
step 2 **built**, step 3 proposed.

Two questions, answered at two scales:

- **Live:** how far behind real time is each stage right now, and is anything
  piling up? Metrics in Prometheus, a Grafana dashboard.
- **Per drive:** once a drive is in, how far behind did it run, and how long
  after it ended was the summary ready? Stored with the drive and shown in the
  app. Traces in Tempo show where the time went for any one second of it.

## What exists today (✅ verified 2026-11-03)

- The cluster runs an OTel collector (`otel-collector.monitoring:4317` gRPC,
  `:4318` HTTP). It exports metrics to Prometheus through its `:8889` endpoint
  (a ServiceMonitor scrapes it) and traces to Tempo. Grafana shows both.
  Dashboards are generated by scripts in
  `infra/cluster-config/monitoring/dashboards/`. Nothing in robo-services
  sends telemetry to it yet.
- Every sample record carries `captured_at` (phone clock: frame base + the
  sample's offset) and `received_at` (kreceiver's clock when it took the frame
  from MQTT), and `payload.base_timestamp_us` names its 1 s frame.
- The phone logs each frame and uploads it over MQTT; frames recorded while
  offline are kept and sent later, so a frame can reach kreceiver minutes or
  hours after it was recorded.
- Waits that exist by design, and so set a floor on the numbers:
  - vehicle-state closes a frame once its receipt's counts are all in, or once
    the topic has moved on (a later frame of the session, any message 30 s
    newer, or 10 s of quiet). A complete frame closes as soon as its last
    sample arrives.
  - drive-summary runs every 60 s and takes a session only once it has ended
    and its last frame arrived 2 or more minutes ago (so late frames still
    count). "Summary ready" is therefore 2–3 minutes after the drive's end at
    best.

## Two lags, kept apart

- **Upload lag** = when kreceiver received the frame − when the frame ended
  (frame base + 1 s). This is the phone and the network. Large values are
  expected after the phone was offline; they are not a pipeline fault.
- **Pipeline lag** = when a stage finished with it − when kreceiver received
  it. This is our code and the number to watch.

Both rely on two clocks (phone and cluster). Phones keep network time to well
under a second, which is fine at the 1 s scale here; sub-second figures across
the phone/cluster boundary are not meaningful.

## 1. Metrics and the dashboard

A small shared module, `can_pub_sub_probe.observability`, sets up OTel metrics
(and later traces) from the standard `OTEL_*` environment, and does nothing
when `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` is unset (tests, local runs). Each service
sets `OTEL_SERVICE_NAME` and the endpoint in its Helm template.

Metrics (seconds, histograms unless noted):

| Metric | Recorded by | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `robo_upload_lag_seconds` | kreceiver, per frame | received − frame end |
| `robo_pipeline_lag_seconds{stage}` | can-decode (per record), vehicle-state (per frame written), session-tracker (per receipt) | done − received |
| `robo_consumer_backlog{reader,topic}` (gauge) | can-decode, session-tracker, vehicle-state | topic size − next offset; absent until the reader's position is known |
| `robo_frames_total{status}` (counter) | vehicle-state | complete / incomplete / empty |

The backlog gauge reuses what the consumer watchdog already reads, and it
stays meaningful when timestamps can't be trusted. A Grafana dashboard,
"robo pipeline", shows the lags (p50/p95), the backlog per topic and frame
outcomes.

Verify: during a drive the dashboard shows upload lag of a few seconds,
pipeline lag per stage, and a backlog near zero; after an iggy restart, the
backlog shows the catch-up.

## 2. Per-drive numbers

vehicle-state gains two columns on each row: `received_at` (the latest
`received_at` among the frame's samples) and `written_at`. They are added by
its own schema setup (`ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS`); old rows stay null.

drive-summary adds a `processing` section to the summary (metrics version 3):

- upload lag p50 / p95 / max;
- pipeline lag (written − received) p50 / p95 / max;
- `summary_after_end_s`: when the summary was written − when the drive ended.

The app shows it under All details as "Processing". Drives summarised before
this have no section and show nothing.

Verify: on a fresh drive, the numbers agree with the dashboard for the same
time range, and `summary_after_end_s` is in the expected 2–3 minutes.

## 3. Traces, one per frame

One trace per 1 s frame (about 600 for a 10 minute drive), not one per
message. The trace and root span IDs are derived from (device, session, frame
base), so every service can attach its span to the right frame without
passing context along: vehicle-state sees a frame's samples from five topics,
and the derivation needs no header plumbing. Spans:

1. `frame` (root): frame start → frame end, on the phone's clock;
2. `upload`: frame end → kreceiver received it;
3. `kreceiver.publish`;
4. `can-decode`: first to last record of the frame decoded;
5. `vehicle-state`: received → row written;
6. `drive-summary`: on the session's last frame only, drive end → summary
   written.

The Flink jobs (race only) are left out for now. Tempo keeps traces for its
configured retention, so traces serve recent drives; the per-drive numbers in
step 2 are the durable record.

Verify: a frame's trace in Grafana shows the spans in order with no gaps
unexplained by the waits above.

## Out of scope

  shows upload progress from the phone's side).
- The Flink jobs and the Rust can-decode experiment; if the Rust decoder
  replaces can-decode it should record the same metric names.
