# 2012 Ford F-150 (planned capture)

Vehicle facts as stated by the owner, 2026-11-24 (not read from a VIN or the door sticker):
3.7 L V6, 6R80 six-speed automatic, XLT, long bed, RWD, 25 gal tank. Only modification: the radio.
No power locks or windows.

Nothing has been captured or decoded yet. Plan: run `tools/dbc_check.py` on the first drive; this
generation is more likely to sit in the CGEA 1.x family (`ford_cgea1_2_ptcan_2011.dbc`, engine message
`0x201`) than the EcoSport's `0x204` family, but that is an expectation, not a finding. Ask for idle time,
several gears, and firm stops in the drive so the rpm and speed checks have something to work with.

## First capture, 2026-11-25 (checker run, local only)

One short drive: 282 s, 68 CAN IDs, 89 s stopped, top speed 75 km/h (phone GPS). Log: session-1795630967022.
Command: `python3 tools/dbc_check.py <log>`.

- **Prediction held:** best fit is `ford_cgea1_2_ptcan_2011.dbc`, 96.0 % of traffic, 63 of 68 IDs. The 5 IDs it lacks are all under 8 Hz, so the discovery list is empty. The base DBC fits only 50.2 %.
- **Speed: PASS, three sources.** `0x201` Veh_V_ActlEng r=0.996 (0.993 km/h per unit); `0x215` wheel speeds r=0.996 (rolling radius 0.379 m, tyre size not read from the truck); `0x415` Veh_V_ActlBrk r=0.995, found through the base DBC.
- **RPM: checker said PLAUSIBLE, but see the follow-up below.** `0x201` EngAout_N_Actl idles at 806 rpm over 88 s stopped. The checker's gear-level test found no structure (top-6 share 0.37 < 0.6), which only means that one test needs a longer drive than this one.
- **Pedal: range and movement only** (0 to 40.2 %, p99 36.9 %), the same limit as on the EcoSport.
- **Gear: found in the follow-up below.**
- **EcoSport DBC carries over almost not at all:** 1 of 68 IDs (`0x415`, speed PASS). The F-150 uses the older 2011-style IDs (`0x201`, `0x215`); the EcoSport uses `0x204`, `0x217`.

## Follow-up, same log: rpm and gear checked directly

The checker's PLAUSIBLE on rpm came from a test that needs gear levels to show up unaided. On a short drive with quick shifts they smear, so I looked at the log directly.

- **RPM behaves like an engine.** It starts at 1270 rpm (cold fast idle) and settles to 760-815 rpm by about 40 s; the owner says low idle on this truck is under 1000 rpm and that rpm looked right during the drive (owner-stated). It also spikes to 3436 rpm during the hard pull at 72 km/h and falls to 1449 rpm once speed holds.
- **Gear signal: `0x151` EngineData_1_CG1 `TrnIpcDsplyGear_D_Actl`** (bit 7, 4 bits, big-endian), 113 Hz. The same signal name and bit position the EcoSport has in `0x171`. Values seen are 1 to 6 only.
- **It follows the driving.** Transitions run 1, 2, 3, 4 on the way up as speed climbs (2 at about 15 km/h, 3 at about 30, 4 at about 43) and 4, 3, 1 on each hard stop. Near the end it goes 3, 4, 5, 6 at 55, 71, 73 and 75 km/h, then 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 as the truck slows.
- **rpm per km/h falls with the gear value**, and the ranges for gears 1 to 4 do not overlap (middle half of the seconds): gear 1 111.6-126.4, gear 2 70.5-87.4, gear 3 41.3-50.8, gear 4 28.3-35.2. Gear 5 (25.0) and 6 (19.6) are further down but rest on only 5 s each, in one acceleration, so they are consistent and thin.
- **Status:** rpm `0x201` is verified for the idle value and for the per-gear ratios in gears 1 to 4. Gear `0x151` is verified for values 1 to 4 and consistent but thinly sampled for 5 and 6. Neither has been checked against a published gear-ratio table.
- **The checker is the weak part.** Its rpm test should accept a cluster-gear signal as a condition when the DBC defines one, and test per-gear ratio separation instead of waiting for levels to appear unaided. That change has not been made.
